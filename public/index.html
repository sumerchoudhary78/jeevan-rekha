When the flood helpline is busy, an AI answers.

In a flood, disaster helplines are flooded too. Jeevan Rekha takes overflow calls in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu and English, asks only what a rescue boat needs, reads numbers back to avoid fatal mishearings, merges duplicate reports and hands human dispatchers a ranked queue.

🗺 Control room

Watch calls being answered live, cases merging, and the rescue queue reprioritising. Includes a flood-night simulation.

How it works

  1. Caller audio streams to Muse Voice Transcribe, biased with the district's village and landmark names, which is what makes noisy phone audio usable.
  2. Muse Spark runs the conversation: one question at a time, in the caller's language, until location, confirmed headcount and vulnerable people are known.
  3. Each report becomes a case. Muse Spark judges whether it duplicates an open case nearby (same people, same building) and merges it, never lowering urgency or headcount.
  4. Cases are ranked by urgency, vulnerable people, headcount and waiting time. Humans assign boats; the AI never refuses or deprioritises anyone.

Built for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon 2026. Demo data only: scripted calls, synthetic voices, no real helpline is connected.
