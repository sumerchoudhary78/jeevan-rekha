ज Jeevan Rekha

When the flood helpline is busy, an AI answers.

In a flood, disaster helplines are flooded too. Jeevan Rekha takes overflow calls in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu and English, asks only what a rescue boat needs, reads numbers back to avoid fatal mishearings, recognises people already reported and hands human dispatchers a ranked queue.

In July 2026 Kosi floodwater cut six villages of Kusheshwar Asthan, Darbhanga, off from the mainland; thousands were stranded (IANS). In Chennai during Cyclone Michaung, residents calling the corporation's flood helpline found no one answering or the lines constantly busy (The News Minute).

How it works

  1. Caller audio streams to Muse Voice Transcribe, biased with the district's village and landmark names, which is what makes noisy phone audio usable.
  2. Muse Spark runs the conversation: one question at a time, in the caller's language, until location, confirmed headcount and vulnerable people are known.
  3. While the caller is on the line, the agent checks the live case board: if a group at the same place (or this phone number) is already reported, it asks once whether the caller is with them and joins the call to that case only on a yes. Recorded calls, which cannot be asked, go to a Muse Spark duplicate judge. Merges never lower urgency or headcount.
  4. Cases are ranked by urgency, vulnerable people, headcount and waiting time. Humans assign boats; the AI never refuses or deprioritises anyone.
